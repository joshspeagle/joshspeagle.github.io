The post skeleton

Every post opens and closes the same way, so a reader always knows where the summary and the practical checklist are. The diagram marks the parts in page order. The Playbook itself is introduced once, in the landing page's about section, and each post points there with its series note.

Color roles

A color names a role, and the role stays the same in every figure, equation and schematic. Figures use at most one focal color at a time. The swatches read the live custom properties, so they change with the theme.

  • --modelModel and profiled curvesSmooth curves from a model, best-case (profiled) results and the main analytical quantity.
  • --dataObserved trialsIndividual noisy runs and measurements, and the Playbook's own data.
  • --reader (--accent)Interactive stateHandles, the selected item and the active tour step. Also the focus ring.
  • --energyFocal highlightThe one item the reader should look at first, and the steps of the current post on the map.
  • --thirdContextNuisance quantities, hardware limits, baselines and fixed slices.
  • --fourthA second seriesA further quantity when a figure needs one, such as a step count beside a time.
  • --contextBackground marksGridlines, reference lines and de-emphasized runs.

Text uses --ink, --ink-2 and --ink-3 in decreasing emphasis, on --bg, --surface and --surface-2. Most color roles also have a darker -label variant for text and a -tint variant for fills.

Light and dark themes

The light theme is a warm paper palette and the dark theme a navy one. The header button cycles through automatic, light and dark, and stores the choice in localStorage under tp-theme. Automatic follows the operating system. Every color in a figure is a custom property, so a figure never has to redraw when the theme changes. To check a page in dark mode from a test script, set localStorage.setItem('tp-theme', 'dark') before the page loads.

Text inside an SVG figure must render at 12 px or more on desktop and 11 px or more on a phone. Size SVG text with the --fs-label and --fs-tick properties and draw the SVG at its natural width, so the text does not shrink with the viewBox.

The figure frame

A figure is built from a fixed anatomy. The author writes the head, the task line, the caption and any extras. site.js numbers the figure, builds a toolbar from the extras it finds, and opens them one at a time in a drawer under the toolbar, so the plot stays visible while the reader compares it with the Playbook's figure or the model's assumptions.

  1. Head figcaption.fig-head: number (added automatically), span.fig-title under about 70 characters, and one span.chip naming the kind of figure.
  2. Task line p.fig-task: one sentence telling the reader what to try.
  3. Toolbar and drawer, built from what the figure contains:
    • Guided tour from div.fig-tutorial[data-figure] and its div.fig-tut-step-data steps;
    • The Playbook's figure from data-playbook-img (or data-playbook-quote), data-playbook-caption and data-playbook-src on the figure;
    • How this toy works from details.fig-model;
    • Data table from details.fig-table.
  4. Controls and panels, drawn by the figure's script into div.fig-panels, then the readout.
  5. Caption p.fig-caption, whose numbers come from the figure's computed quantities through span[data-num].

The guided tour is closed when the page loads, so the figure starts in the state the caption describes. Opening the tour applies step 1, the dots and the Back and Next buttons move between steps (the arrow keys work too, and Escape closes it), and closing the tour resets the figure. Each step sets a preset (data-preset), parameters (data-params, JSON) or a run position (data-t). data-t counts the run's own steps, here the 20 evaluations, and the tour logs an error if it exceeds the run's limit. Every step should change what is on screen, and its two or three sentences should describe the new state.

Figure chips use three kinds: Interactive toy model for simulations, Schematic for static diagrams and Playbook data for the Playbook's own measurements.

Width classes: a figure is 840 px wide by default, l-wide makes it 1080 px for multi-panel layouts and wide diagrams, and l-text keeps a small figure in the 680-px column. On phones every figure runs edge to edge. A static diagram uses figure.schematic with the same head and caption.

Components in the text

Concept terms

A term with a definition is shaded, with a dotted underline and a help cursor, so it does not look like a link. Hovering, focusing or tapping it opens a short definition with links to the post that explains it and to the Playbook. In this sentence, retrospective checkpoint selection and a nuisance hyperparameter are concept terms. Mark the first occurrence of a term in a section, and keep them sparse in summaries.

Math asides and equations

Main-text equations are introduced in words first, with every symbol defined where it appears. A symbol that a figure also draws takes that figure's color through \htmlClass{math-col-model}{…} (and math-col-accent, -third, -fourth, -data). For example, the wall-clock time to reach a target is the product of the step latency and the number of steps,

$$\htmlClass{math-col-model}{T(B)} = \htmlClass{math-col-third}{t_{\text{step}}(B)} \times \htmlClass{math-col-fourth}{S(B)},$$

where $T(B)$ is the training time at batch size $B$, $t_{\text{step}}(B)$ the time per step and $S(B)$ the number of steps. No display equation may need a horizontal scrollbar in the 680-px column. Derivations, edge cases and technical framing go in a collapsible aside:

Why the best of many noisy trials is optimistic

If each trial's score is its true error plus independent noise, the minimum over $K$ trials picks the trial whose noise happened to be most negative. The expected minimum therefore sits below the true error of the chosen configuration, and the gap grows with $K$.

Scrubbable numbers

An inline calculator lets the reader change a number in a sentence and see the result update. Each adjustable number has − and + buttons, a drag zone marked ⇄, and a bar along its bottom edge showing where the value sits in its range. It also takes focus and responds to the arrow keys. Use one only where changing the number teaches something, and make sure the sentence still reads well as plain text.

Trial budget Drag a number, or use − and +

Comparing 3 candidates, each with a learning-rate range of 3.0 decades sampled at 5 trials per decade, needs 15 trials per candidate and 45 trials in total.

Callouts

Callouts hold material that sits beside the argument. Use at most two or three in a post. The four variants differ by the color of the left rule and the head.

Concept

A definition or idea the reader needs before the next step.

From the literature

A mechanism or result from a cited paper that goes beyond the Playbook.

Worked numbers

A short calculation with the numbers spelled out.

Common mistake

A failure the Playbook warns about, with how to recognize it.

The tuning-process map

The map lays out the Playbook's process in four phases (set up the project, plan a round, read the results, decide and start the next round) with 19 steps. Each step carries the number of the post that covers it. On a container at least 760 px wide it draws a flowchart with an inspector below. On a narrower container it becomes a list of the same steps grouped by phase, each of which opens in place. Both views share one selection.

The specimen below starts on the adoption decision. Click a step, or tab to it and press Enter. The arrow keys move between steps in order, and the buttons under "Answer" or "Next" follow the chart.

  • On a post no attributes are needed. The map reads body[data-page], marks the steps that post covers in the focal color, and selects the first of them.
  • data-default-node selects a particular step on load. Step ids are s_arch, s_opt, s_batch, s_init, s_pipe, p_goal, p_roles, p_space, p_search, r_score, r_curves, r_instab, r_space, r_iso, d_adopt, d_yes, d_no, d_explore and d_final. Older ids such as d_gain are mapped to their new steps. On a post, a step that post does not cover is ignored.
  • data-active-zone outlines one phase (setup, plan, read or decide) on pages that are not posts. Older zone names are mapped.
  • Reading progress: opening a post adds its number to localStorage under tp-posts-opened, and the map fills the badges of posts the reader has opened.
